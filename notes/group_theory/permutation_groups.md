### Permutation Groups

##### Permutations

A permutation of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is a bijection

$$
\sigma:\{1,\ldots,n\}\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,n\}.
$$

Its two-line notation is

$$
\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&n\\\sigma(1)&\sigma(2)&\cdots&\sigma(n)\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The columns may be reordered without changing the permutation.

Permutation products act from right to left:

$$
(\sigma\tau)(i)=\sigma\!\left(\tau(i)\right).
$$

The inverse exchanges the two rows:

$$
\sigma^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma(1)&\sigma(2)&\cdots&\sigma(n)\\1&2&\cdots&n\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The $n!$ permutations of $n$ objects form the permutation group $S_n$:

$$
\boxed{|S_n|=n!}.
$$

##### Cycles and Transpositions

A cycle of length $\ell$ is

$$
(a_1\,a_2\,\cdots\,a_\ell),
$$

which maps

$$
a_1\mapsto a_2,\quad a_2\mapsto a_3,\quad\ldots,\quad a_\ell\mapsto a_1,
$$

and fixes all remaining objects.

- Cyclic symmetry

$$
(a_1\,a_2\,\cdots\,a_\ell)=(a_2\,\cdots\,a_\ell\,a_1).
$$

- Inverse

$$
(a_1\,a_2\,\cdots\,a_\ell)^{-1}=(a_\ell\,\cdots\,a_2\,a_1).
$$

- Disjoint cycles commute

$$
(a_1\,\cdots\,a_\ell)(b_1\,\cdots\,b_m)=(b_1\,\cdots\,b_m)(a_1\,\cdots\,a_\ell).
$$

- Cycles sharing one object can be joined

$$
(a_1\,\cdots\,a_\ell\,c)(c\,b_1\,\cdots\,b_m)=(a_1\,\cdots\,a_\ell\,c\,b_1\,\cdots\,b_m).
$$

- A cycle is a product of transpositions

$$
(a_1\,a_2\,\cdots\,a_\ell)=(a_1\,a_\ell)\cdots(a_1\,a_3)(a_1\,a_2).
$$

Every permutation decomposes uniquely into disjoint cycles, up to the order of the cycles and cyclic symmetry within each cycle.

Counting fixed points as $1$-cycles, let $r$ be the number of disjoint cycles, $\lambda_i$ the length of the $i$-th cycle, and $m_\ell$ the number of cycles of length $\ell$. The cycle structure is the partition

$$
\boxed{\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)\equiv(1^{m_1}2^{m_2}\cdots n^{m_n}),\qquad \lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_r>0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ell m_\ell=n}.
$$

A cycle of length $2$ is a transposition.

##### Conjugacy Classes and Parity

Conjugation relabels the objects in each cycle:

$$
\tau(a_1\,a_2\,\cdots\,a_\ell)\tau^{-1}=\left(\tau(a_1)\,\tau(a_2)\,\cdots\,\tau(a_\ell)\right).
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{\sigma\sim\tau\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sigma,\tau\text{ have the same cycle structure}}.
$$

The conjugacy classes of $S_n$ are therefore labeled by the partitions of $n$. For the cycle structure

$$
\lambda=(1^{m_1}2^{m_2}\cdots n^{m_n}),
$$

the class size is

$$
|C_\lambda|=\frac{n!}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\ell^{m_\ell}m_\ell!}.
$$

Every permutation is a product of transpositions. Although the decomposition is not unique, the parity of the number of transpositions is unique. Define

$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}+1,&\sigma\text{ is even},\\-1,&\sigma\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}
$$

Then

$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\tau)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{sgn}(\tau).
$$

The even permutations form the alternating group

$$
\boxed{A_n=\ker(\operatorname{sgn}),\qquad |A_n|=\frac{n!}{2}}.
$$

##### Generators of $S_n$

- Every permutation is a product of transpositions.

- Every transposition is a product of adjacent transpositions:

$$
(a\,\,a+k)=(a\,\,a+1)\cdots(a+k-1\,\,a+k)\cdots(a\,\,a+1).
$$

- Let $c=(1\,2\,\cdots\,n)$. Every adjacent transposition is generated by $(1\,2)$ and $c$:

$$
(a\,\,a+1)=c^{a-1}(1\,2)c^{-(a-1)}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{S_n=\langle(1\,2),(1\,2\,\cdots\,n)\rangle}.
$$

##### Cayley’s Theorem

Let $G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_n\}$ be a finite group. Left multiplication by $g\in G$ permutes its elements:

$$
\rho(g)=\begin{pmatrix}g_1&g_2&\cdots&g_n\\gg_1&gg_2&\cdots&gg_n\end{pmatrix}\in S_n.
$$

The map $\rho:G\to S_n$ satisfies

$$
\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h),\qquad \ker\rho=\{e\}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{G\simeq\rho(G)\leq S_n}.
$$

Every finite group of order $n$ is thus isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_n$.
