### Green Functions

##### Definition

Let $\mathcal L$ be a linear operator and consider the inhomogeneous equation

$$
\mathcal L u(x)=f(x).
$$

Under specified boundary conditions, the Green function is the integral kernel of $\mathcal L^{-1}$:

$$
G(x,x')=\langle x|\mathcal L^{-1}|x'\rangle,\qquad \mathcal L_xG(x,x')=\delta(x-x').
$$

The solution generated by the source $f(x)$ is

$$
\boxed{u(x)=\int dx'\,G(x,x')f(x')},\qquad \mathcal L u(x)=\int dx'\,\delta(x-x')f(x')=f(x).
$$

Thus, $G(x,x')$ is the response at $x$ to a unit source at $x'$. It is determined jointly by $\mathcal L$ and the boundary conditions; if $\mathcal L$ has zero modes, its inverse must be restricted to the compatible subspace.

### Examples

##### Poisson Equation

The electrostatic potential satisfies

$$
-\nabla^2\Phi(\mathbf r)=\frac{\rho(\mathbf r)}{\epsilon_0}.
$$

Define the Green function by

$$
-\nabla_{\mathbf r}^2G(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r-\mathbf r'),\qquad G(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=\frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}.
$$

The potential is therefore

$$
\boxed{\Phi(\mathbf r)=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int d^3\mathbf r'\,\frac{\rho(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}}.
$$

Under the Fourier transform,

$$
q^2G(\mathbf q)=1,\qquad G(\mathbf q)=\frac{1}{q^2}.
$$

##### Quantum Green Function

Let the Hamiltonian be decomposed into a solvable part and an interaction:

$$
\hat H=\hat H_0+\hat V,\qquad (\hat H_0+\hat V)|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle.
$$

The stationary Schrödinger equation becomes

$$
(E-\hat H_0)|\psi\rangle=\hat V|\psi\rangle.
$$

Define the free resolvent and its kernel by

$$
\hat G_0(E)=(E-\hat H_0)^{-1},\qquad G_0(x,x';E)=\langle x|\hat G_0(E)|x'\rangle.
$$

When $E$ lies in the continuous spectrum of $\hat H_0$, define

$$
\boxed{\hat G_0^\pm(E)=\lim_{\epsilon\to0^+}\frac{1}{E-\hat H_0\pm i\epsilon}}.
$$

Its spectral representation is

$$
\hat G_0^\pm(E)=\sum_n\frac{|n\rangle\langle n|}{E-E_n\pm i0}+\int dE'\,\frac{|E'\rangle\langle E'|}{E-E'\pm i0}.
$$

In scattering theory, $\hat G_0^+(E)$ selects the outgoing-wave solution, while $\hat G_0^-(E)$ selects the incoming-wave solution.

##### Full Green Function

For $\hat H=\hat H_0+\hat V$, define the full and free Green functions by

$$
\hat G^\pm(E)=\frac{1}{E-\hat H\pm i0},\qquad \hat G_0^\pm(E)=\frac{1}{E-\hat H_0\pm i0}.
$$

Using

$$
(A-B)^{-1}=A^{-1}+A^{-1}B(A-B)^{-1},
$$

with $A=E-\hat H_0\pm i0$ and $B=\hat V$ gives

$$
\boxed{\hat G^\pm(E)=\hat G_0^\pm(E)+\hat G_0^\pm(E)\hat V\hat G^\pm(E)}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\hat G^\pm(E)=\hat G_0^\pm(E)+\hat G^\pm(E)\hat V\hat G_0^\pm(E).
$$

Iterating the resolvent identity yields

$$
\hat G^\pm=\hat G_0^\pm+\hat G_0^\pm\hat V\hat G_0^\pm+\hat G_0^\pm\hat V\hat G_0^\pm\hat V\hat G_0^\pm+\cdots.
$$

The resolvent identity is exact; the series representation requires convergence.
